<div class="max-w-5xl mx-auto">
  <div class="titulo text-center">What we propose</div>
  <div class="cita text-center" style="font-size: 1.7rem">An international agreement to pause the training of new frontier models until there are safety guarantees that can be verified.</div>
  <div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-6 mt-6 text-lg">
    <div v-click="1" class="tarjeta">
      <div class="font-bold mb-2">The AI you already use stays</div>
      What stops is the race for the next generation.
    </div>
    <div v-click="1" class="tarjeta">
      <div class="font-bold mb-2">It's been done before</div>
      In the middle of the Cold War, the US and the USSR limited their nuclear weapons and accepted inspections. And with the Montreal Protocol, every country banned the gases that were destroying the ozone layer.
    </div>
  </div>

  - Nuclear: the Non-Proliferation Treaty (1968) and the arms limitation treaties between the US

    Answers "what about China?": rival powers have agreed on limits before when the risk was
    everyone's. Honest weak spot: it didn't fully prevent proliferation (there are nine
    nuclear-armed states) and several treaties have expired. It reduced and contained the risk,
    it didn't eliminate it.
  - Montreal Protocol (1987): phased out the CFCs that were destroying the ozone layer. Ratified by
    every country; the layer is recovering. A profitable industry regulated in time thanks to
    scientific warnings, and it kept working (we still have fridges).
